# GradQuest V2.32: Evaluation & Improvement Report

## 1. Overview of Changes (V2.27 → V2.32)
GradQuest V2.32 is a significant leap forward in terms of both **UX clarity** and **gameplay depth**. The developer has clearly listened to feedback, addressing several critical friction points while adding new strategic layers that make the "PhD struggle" feel even more authentic.

### Key Improvements
| Feature | Change in V2.32 | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Specialization Selection** | Added bright borders and "Selected" labels. | **High**: Eliminates initial confusion on the start screen. |
| **Conference System** | Replaced single-click with interactive choices (Talk, Poster, Mixer). | **High**: Adds meaningful strategic decisions and risk/reward. |
| **Graduation Progress** | Introduced a phased approach (e.g., Phase 1: Literature Review). | **Medium**: Provides better short-term goals for the player. |
| **Strategic Exits** | Added "Master's Exit" and other career pivot options. | **Medium**: Gives players a graceful way to end a failing run. |
| **Emergency Options** | "Medical Leave" and "Take Time Off" are now more prominent. | **High**: Prevents sudden game overs and adds resource management. |

---

## 2. Gameplay Observations

### 2.1 The "Scooped" Mechanic
The introduction of the "Scooped" status effect is a brilliant thematic addition. It forces the player to consider the **Pre-Register Idea** action, creating a tension between spending "Network" points for safety versus saving them for other bonuses. This adds a much-needed layer of counter-play to the random events.

### 2.2 Balance & Difficulty
The game remains **extremely difficult**. In a 49-month run, I was unable to complete a single publication despite a balanced strategy. While this captures the "long haul" nature of a PhD, the early game might still feel a bit slow for new players. The "Phase 1: Literature Review" goal helps, but the transition to "Phase 2" could be made slightly more rewarding.

---

## 3. Suggestions for Future Improvements

### 3.1 UX & Visual Feedback
- **Research Pipeline Tooltips**: While the icons are clear, hovering over "Discovery" or "Figures" could show a small tooltip explaining exactly what is needed to progress (e.g., "Requires 3 Figures to submit").
- **Advisor Personality Clues**: The "Advisor System" mentions hidden traits. Providing subtle visual or text clues in the "What Happened" feed (e.g., "Your advisor seems particularly focused on rigor this month") would make the **Pitch Session** feel more like a detective mechanic.

### 3.2 Gameplay Mechanics
- **Early Game "Quick Wins"**: Consider adding a "Workshop Paper" option—a very short (2-month) pipeline that gives a small morale boost and a tiny bit of "Network," but doesn't count toward the 3-paper graduation requirement. This would give players an early sense of accomplishment.
- **Network Milestones**: The help modal lists bonuses at 40, 60, and 80 Network. These should be **explicitly tracked** in the main UI (perhaps a small badge next to the Network score) so players know how close they are to the next "level up."
- **Exhaustion Clarity**: The "Exhaustion" status effect is very punishing. Adding a "Recovery Progress" bar to the status effect card would help players plan exactly how many "Take a Break" actions they need to clear it.

### 3.3 Narrative & Variety
- **Field-Specific Events**: While specializations have unique actions, adding **field-specific random events** (e.g., "A new theorem disproves your lemma" for Theoreticians) would further differentiate the playstyles.

---

## 4. Conclusion
GradQuest V2.32 is in an excellent state. It has transitioned from a simple resource management game into a nuanced simulation with genuine strategic depth. The new conference system and strategic exits are standout features that significantly improve the player experience. With a few more "quality of life" tweaks to feedback and early-game pacing, it will be a truly top-tier browser simulation.
